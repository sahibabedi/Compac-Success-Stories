Case Study 08 ITW Chemin

Coolant Management
for a Reputed Auto-Ancillary Plant

An auto-ancillary manufacturer was dealing with rust on machined parts, high coolant costs and short tool life. For almost five years, our service engineers have maintained the coolant across its plants every single day, delivering rust-free components.

Project Lead Inderjit Singh Sahota
Industry
Auto-Ancillary Manufacturing
Application
Metalworking Fluid Management
Product
ITW Biocool 80 Coolant
Customer
A major auto-ancillary OEM in North India*
01

The Customer

What they make

Precision-Machined Auto Components

The customer machines components for automotive and tractor OEMs, including rocker arms, connecting rods, shifter forks, shafts and levers.

The plants

Multiple Plants in North India

The company runs several plants in North India. Together they have more than 300 machines running on water-soluble coolant.

The machines

VMCs, CNC Lathes,
SPMs and Grinders

Each machine has its own coolant sump, from 20 litres on the smallest grinders to 1,000 litres on the largest machining centres. The plants hold over 75,000 litres of coolant between them.

02

The Problem

  • ARust was appearing on machined components. A component that rusted after machining had to be reworked or rejected.
  • BThe plants wanted to optimize their coolant costs. With hundreds of sumps to charge, top up and replace, the spend added up quickly.
  • CTool life needed to improve. Tools wore early, which raised tooling costs and led to frequent stoppages for tool changes.
03

Our Analysis

Our team studied the current operating process at each plant.

What we found

There was no documented process for handling coolant, and no standard SOPs. Checks for pH, tramp oil and bacterial growth were not carried out. Each plant followed a different process, which led to coolant being wasted.

01

Streamlining the Coolant Process

Our team mapped every machine at each plant with its type, operation and sump size. This mapping helped us evaluate the ideal coolant consumption for each plant and identify areas of improvement.

02

Concentration by Operation

Machining and grinding need coolant at different strengths. For each machine, the team set a recommended concentration and a top-up ratio based on the operation it ran.

03

Water Loss and Top-Ups

The team estimated how much water the sumps lost every day, and how much concentrate the top-ups needed to maintain the coolant at strength.

04

Running Cost

The team compared the cost of running each machine on coolant, counting the first charge, monthly top-ups, sump life and the disposal of spent coolant.

What we concluded

Rust, coolant cost and tool life all depend on a coolant that suits the job and is maintained at the right concentration in every sump. With more than 300 sumps across the plants, that called for one documented process at every plant and an engineer on site to monitor the coolant every day.

04

Our Solution

We recommended ITW Chemin’s Biocool 80, a water-soluble coolant, and took on the daily management of the coolant at the customer’s plants.

Our engineers standardized coolant handling under one coolant management system, so every sump at every plant is handled the same way.

Daily coolant management on site

01

An engineer at every plant, every day. For almost five years, our engineers have been present at the customer’s plants every single day to monitor and manage the coolant.

02

Daily monitoring. Every day, the engineers check each sump’s concentration, pH, tramp oil and bacterial growth. They carry out the water and coolant top-ups that hold each sump at its set concentration and reduce wastage.

03

One documented process. Our coolant management system gave every plant the same documented procedure and SOPs for handling coolant.

04

Cleaning and recharging. Machines are cleaned and charged with fresh coolant whenever a sump needs it.

05

Monthly records. We monitor coolant performance and submit coolant records to the customer every month.

06

Oil skimming. Oil skimming was a new service for the plants. We installed oil skimmers that remove tramp oil from the sump surface before it can spoil the coolant.

07

Dosing pumps. We installed dosing pumps, so every top-up of water and concentrate goes into the sump at the set ratio.

What daily management changed

  • 01Longer coolant life and less downtime. The coolant now lasts longer between full sump changes, so machines stop less often for sumps to be drained, cleaned and recharged.
  • 02Lower sump disposal costs. Fewer full sump changes mean the plants have less spent coolant to dispose of.
  • 03No Monday morning smell. Coolant left untouched over a weekend usually starts to smell by Monday, because bacteria grow in it while the machines are off. At these plants, the coolant stays free of bacteria even when a machine stands idle for up to 15 days with nothing done to the sump. With little to no bacteria, operators who handle the parts and the coolant also face a lower risk of dermatitis.
