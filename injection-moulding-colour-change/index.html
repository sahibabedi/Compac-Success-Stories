Less material wastage during colour change in injection moulding

Our application review and introduction of Ultra Purge™ 1215T helped a home appliance manufacturer reduce colour-change waste and turn around its machine back into production sooner.

01

The Customer

What they make

Home Appliances

The customer manufactures home appliances, with injection moulding used to produce plastic components. Switching between colours requires the previous colour to be purged from the machine before the next production run.

The machine

Injection Moulding Machine

Plastic is melted and conveyed through a heated barrel by a rotating screw, then injected into a mould. During colour changes, residual material within the machine needs to be removed to prevent colour carryover.

The challenge

Difficult Colour Changes

The plant was using its own processing polymer to purge the machine. Colour changes consumed excessive material and took too long to complete.

Cost of the problem

Wasted Material and Production Time

Material used to flush out the previous colour added to changeover waste. Extended purging also kept the machine occupied for longer before it could resume productive moulding.

02

The Problem

  • APurging with the plant’s processing polymer generated substantial material wastage during colour changes.
  • BCleaning the machine took considerable time, extending the purging stage between production runs.
  • CThe combined loss of material and machine time was affecting productivity and cost.
03

Our Analysis

We reviewed the colour-change application and the customer’s existing polymer-based purging practice to identify where a dedicated purging solution could improve the process.

01

Existing Purging Method

The plant relied on its processing polymer to purge the previous colour. This put the focus on how effectively the purging material could clean the machine and how much material was consumed in doing so.

02

Where the Colour Stays

Melt moves through the barrel and nozzle in layers, fastest at the centre of each passage and slowest against the steel. A film of the previous colour stays on the barrel wall, the screw flights, the check ring and the nozzle after the next material has started to flow.

03

Material and Time Losses

The customer identified two linked problems: the quantity of material wasted and the time needed to purge the machine. An effective solution needed to address both, reducing waste while allowing production to resume sooner.

04

Application-Specific Selection

Based on our review, we selected Ultra Purge™ 1215T for the injection moulding colour-change application. The recommendation targeted the purging step responsible for the customer’s material and time losses.

What we concluded

The material and time losses came from the purging method. A processing polymer is made to be moulded, and it removes the film of previous colour gradually, so purging the machine consumed a large quantity of polymer and needed a long cleaning stage. Improving the purging method offered a direct opportunity to reduce changeover losses. Our recommendation was to introduce a dedicated purging compound that could purge residual colour more efficiently and reduce the material consumed in clearing the machine.

04

Our Solution

We introduced Chem-Trend Ultra Purge™ 1215T into the customer’s colour-change process to address the limitations of purging with their processing polymer.

Dedicated purging compounds are formulated to help remove residual material and colour from plastics processing equipment. Their cleaning action helps reduce the amount of material needed to clear the machine, shortening the purging stage between production runs.

Our work brought together the application review, product selection and use of the selected compound on the customer’s machine. The reported outcome was an up to 80% reduction in material wastage compared with the previous polymer-based purging method. Purging time also decreased, helping improve productivity.
