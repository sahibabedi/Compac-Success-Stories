Case Study 06 Klüber Lubrication

Cutting Grease Consumption by 50% in Cattle Feed Pellet Mills

Bearing failures and frequent re-greasing had become part of the maintenance routine at a cattle feed plant. The plant approached Compac to assess the application and understand why the existing lubrication approach was falling short.

Project Lead Dinesh Kumar
Industry
Animal Feed Manufacturing
Application
Pellet-Mill Bearings
Product
Klüberplex MH 41‑681 BH
Customer
A cattle feed manufacturer in Punjab
01

The Customer

What they make

Cattle Feed

The customer manufactures compound cattle feed in pellet form, and its pellet mills are central to that production. The plant’s output depends on keeping them running.

The process

Conditioning and Pelleting

The feed mash is first conditioned with steam, which softens it and helps the pellets bind. The mash then enters the pellet mill hot and moist, where it is pressed into pellets.

The equipment

Pellet Mills with Tapered Roller Bearings

The application involved tapered roller bearings operating under high loads and elevated temperatures. A rotating ring die carries the mashed feed past a set of rollers, which press it through holes in the die to form pellets.

02

The Problem

  • AThe pellet-mill bearings were failing on a regular basis. They would heat up very quickly, and oil would visibly leak out.
  • BRe-greasing required regular equipment stoppages. The maintenance team re-greased the bearings twice a day, and each round took at least half an hour.
  • CThe bearings worked under high temperatures and heavy load. The plant was using substantial quantities of AP3 grease to maintain the application.
03

Our Analysis

01

Bearing Inspection

Compac’s engineers observed that the bearings were running very hot, with visible oil separation from the grease. These findings prompted a closer review of lubricant suitability and application practices.

02

Greasing Practice

The maintenance team’s routine was to pump grease until it emerged from the other side, so the stopping point was set by visible discharge, without a measured replenishment quantity. Our engineers explained to the team that excess grease retained around the rolling elements can churn, generating additional heat and accelerating lubricant deterioration.

03

Grease Suitability

The team checked how well the thickener in AP3 holds on to its base oil at the temperature these bearings reach.

04

Failure Mechanism

The team traced how heat, overfilling and oil loss combined to affect the rolling contacts and the plant’s grease use.

What we concluded

Two problems were feeding each other. The first was the grease. At the bearings’ temperature and load, the thickener in AP3 released its base oil too quickly, and the oil drained away, leaving the rolling contacts without a protective film. The second was the greasing practice, as the extra heat from overfilled bearings made the oil bleeding worse. Together, the two caused the repeated failures and the high grease consumption.

04

Our Solution

Compac recommended Klüberplex MH 41-681 BH to replace AP3 in the pellet-mill bearings, and corrected the way the grease was applied. This high-temperature grease is made with a special oil and a lithium complex soap thickener.

The lithium complex thickener gives the grease good resistance to oxidation at high temperature and good stability under mechanical working, so it keeps its structure and holds its oil in the conditions where AP3 had been losing it.

Its base oil has a viscosity of about 680 mm²/s at 40 °C. A heavy base oil of this kind builds a thicker film between the rollers and races under load, and the grease carries a four-ball weld load of 2000 N. At NLGI grade 1 it is soft enough to pump, which suits bearings that are re-greased regularly.

Correcting the greasing practice

01

The right quantity. Compac’s engineers worked out the correct grease quantity for each bearing from its outer diameter and width, and clearly labelled each grease point.

02

A practical benchmark. They then calibrated the grease gun on site. By weighing the gun before and after greasing one bearing, and timing the fill, they set a simple measure that operators can follow day to day.

Trial and adoption

03

Trial. We conducted a 15-day trial of Klüberplex MH 41-681 BH on the pellet-mill bearings.

04

Adoption. After a successful trial, the plant replaced AP3 with Klüberplex MH 41-681 BH on its pellet mills and continued to use it.
